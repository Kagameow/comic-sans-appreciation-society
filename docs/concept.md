# The Great Migration — Concept & Flow

## What it is

An office-wide, day-long celebration of Vue 3 release day (May 28th), dressed
up as a treasure hunt. The whole office becomes the game board. Every desk,
coffee machine, plant, and meeting room is a potential hiding spot. The goal
is to turn a normally invisible event — a framework version bump — into a
shared, slightly chaotic, intensely competitive party.

The vibe is part scavenger hunt, part pub quiz, part office sports day, with
a single dramatic finale that everyone watches happen live on a TV.

## The cast

- **Players** — anyone in the office. They hunt for codes, play minigames,
  win IRL matches, and keep one eye on the leaderboard.
- **Architects (admins)** — the people running the day. They hand out
  victory codes after IRL games, tweak the multiplier when energy dips, and
  hold the keys to the Super Code.
- **The TV** — a leaderboard running in the corner of the room. It's the
  public scoreboard, the broadcast channel, and the heartbeat of the day.

## What players do

There are three things going on at once, and players can dip in and out of
any of them.

### 1. Code Check — the steady grind

Codes are stickers, slips, and printouts hidden in plain sight around the
office. A player finds one, walks up to the Code Check page, types it in.
Depending on what kind of code it is, one of a few things happens:

- **Plain point code** → some points land in their pile, a gem lights up.
- **Minigame code** → a small puzzle pops open (a trivia question, a
  crossword, a challenge card). Solve it, get points.
- **Victory code** → handed out by an architect after a real-world game
  (darts, foosball, ping pong, whatever the office runs). Bumps a separate
  victory counter and pays out a chunky base reward.
- **Invalid / already-used** → a gentle rejection, no harm done.

Every redemption nudges one or both of the two progress tracks that matter:
**gems** and **victories**.

### 2. The 5 Vue 3 Gems

A parallel collectible track. Every point code or solved minigame fills in
another gem. Five gems lights up the clue. It's the "I'm playing casually,
I'm just here for the snacks" path to the finale.

### 3. The IRL victories

The competitive path. Win five real-world matches — get five victory codes
from the architects — and the clue lights up the same way. This is for the
players who'd rather slam a foosball than solve a crossword.

## The chaos multiplier

At any point an architect can flip on a multiplier — 2x, 3x, whatever feels
right. While it's live, every point earned anywhere in the office is
amplified. It's a deliberate tool for shaping the day: punch it on when
energy is flagging, kill it before someone runs away with the leaderboard,
turn it into a "happy hour" thing.

Super Code rewards ignore the multiplier — see below.

## The Master Branch — the finale

The whole day builds toward this.

1. **The grind.** A player accumulates gems or wins matches.
2. **The unlock.** The moment either track hits 5, a cryptic clue appears
   on their Code Check page — the **Master Branch clue**. Only that player
   sees it. Nobody else knows it's been unlocked.
3. **The search.** The clue points to a physical sticker hidden somewhere
   in the office: the **Super Code**. The player has to leave their desk
   and actually go find it.
4. **The merge.** They come back, type the code in, and —
5. **The broadcast.** The TV in the corner takes over the room. A full
   "MASTER BRANCH MERGED" overlay plays. Everyone looks up. The winner is
   named. The day's grand prize lands.

The Super Code is **single-use, first-come-first-served**. Only one player
in the office wins it. Everyone else who unlocks the clue still has to
race — and most of them will lose.

If the physical sticker gets lost, stolen, or eaten by the office dog, an
architect can quietly swap which code counts as the Super Code and reset
the winner. The show goes on.

## The shape of the day

- **Morning** — slow start. Codes get found. The leaderboard fills in.
  Gems trickle. A few quick minigames.
- **Midday** — IRL games kick off. Victory codes start flowing.
  Architects flip the multiplier on for the lunch crowd.
- **Afternoon** — the leaders start hitting 5. Clues unlock in private.
  The hunt for the Super Code begins. Nobody admits they have the clue.
- **The moment** — someone finds the sticker. They type it in. The TV
  explodes. The room reacts. The day has its winner.
- **After** — the leaderboard stays up for the rest of the day for
  bragging rights and second-place sulking.

## Why it works

- **Two paths to the finale** means casual players and competitive players
  both have a shot. Nobody is locked out.
- **Server-side scoring** means no arguments about who earned what — the
  scoreboard is the truth.
- **The Super Code is physical** so the climax pulls people out of their
  chairs and into the office.
- **The TV broadcast** turns a private win into a shared moment. The whole
  office watches the same thing at the same time.
- **The architects have real levers** (multiplier, point adjustments,
  swappable Super Code) so the day can be tuned in real time rather than
  hoping the rules survive contact with reality.
